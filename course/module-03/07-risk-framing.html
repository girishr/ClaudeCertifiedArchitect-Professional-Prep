Risk framing

Four functions, one register, and a signature that is not yours to give.

The controls are built and some risk is left over. That leftover is the thing you have to name, size and hand to somebody, and the last move in this domain is knowing who that somebody is.

NIST's AI Risk Management Framework gives the shape. Four functions, one of which is not a stage at all.

The four functions and what each one produces
FunctionWhat it covers
GovernPolicy, roles, accountability, culture. Cuts across the other three rather than sitting alongside them
MapContext, use case, stakeholders, what could go wrong
MeasureEvals, metrics, red teaming, quantifying residual risk
ManagePrioritise, treat, monitor, respond

If an item asks which function is cross-cutting, it is Govern. If an item describes a team that has done evals but has no named owner and no policy, it is Govern that is missing, not more measurement.

7.1The register, and the signature

The practical artefact is a risk register, and its columns are the examinable part: owner, a severity multiplied by likelihood score, inherent risk against residual risk, control mapping, and an accepted-risk sign-off by a named business owner.

That last column is the one the exam cares about. Risk acceptance is a business decision, not an architect's decision. The architect's job is to make the residual risk legible: here is what is left, here is how big it is, here is what it would cost to reduce further. Signing it off is somebody else's authority.

Watch out

If a stem has an architect unilaterally accepting residual risk, that is the wrong answer, however sensible the rest of the option sounds. The same goes for an option where the risk is simply not recorded: an unregistered risk has not been accepted by anyone, and nobody can reverse a decision that was never written down.

Inherent against residual is worth holding separately, because it is what lets you defend the proportionality argument from section 2. Inherent risk is what the use case carries before controls. Residual is what survives them. The gap between the two is the value of your stack, and it is the number that tells a business owner whether the next control is worth buying.

Where this joins up. Rollback, undo, kill switch, canary and an overturnable human decision are all the same property expressed at different layers. A design that keeps them is a design whose residual risk is small enough for someone to sign.

Frame the risk so it can be owned. Size it, map it to controls, state what is left, and put the name of the person who accepted it on the page.
