A security review asks three questions: is our data used for training, how long is it kept, and where does it sit. You need the answers cold, because every one of them has a default, an exception, and a distractor built on confusing the two.

6.1Training, retention and ZDR

By default Anthropic does not use commercial product inputs or outputs to train models. That covers the API, Claude for Work and Claude Gov. Exceptions are opt-in: thumbs feedback, which retains the related conversation for up to five years and may be used for training, and programmes such as the Development Partner Program. Consumer plans, Free, Pro and Max, run under a separate regime. On the API, Anthropic is a processor and you are the controller.

Commercial API conversation content is not retained by default. Zero data retention goes further and is narrower than people assume.

What ZDR covers and does not cover
Scope
CoveredThe Messages and Token Counting APIs on eligible features, Claude Code with commercial API keys, and Claude Platform on AWS

Watch out

ZDR is not the healthcare answer. For PHI the arrangement is a HIPAA-ready configuration with a BAA, meaning encryption, access control and audit logging. ZDR's immediate deletion destroys exactly the auditability HIPAA wants. This pair, PHI in the stem and ZDR in the options, is a classic and it appears often.

6.2Certifications, and what they buy you

SOC 2 Type I and II, ISO/IEC 27001:2022, ISO/IEC 42001:2023 for AI management systems, and a HIPAA-ready configuration with BAAs. Evidence comes through the Trust Center. All of it applies to the commercial products, not to consumer plans.

What it buys you is inherited evidence for your own audit, and nothing more. "Anthropic is SOC 2 certified so we are compliant" is a wrong answer in every form it appears in. A vendor attestation covers the vendor. Your application's controls are still yours to build and evidence.

6.3Residency and deployment

On the first-party API there are two independent settings, and confusing them is the trap. inference_geo sets where inference runs, per request, on Claude 4.6 and later models. Values are "global", the default, or "us"; US pinning costs 1.1x. Workspace geo governs storage at rest and endpoint processing, is fixed at workspace creation, and is currently "us" only. Legacy global-routing opt-outs were auto-migrated to allowed_inference_geos: ["us"].

On Bedrock and Vertex the parameter does not apply at all. Region comes from the endpoint or the inference profile.

The sentence to memorise

Deployment choice changes who you contract with, which audit evidence you inherit, and where data sits. It does not change model behaviour, and it does not reduce your obligation to build guardrails. Any option that treats a deployment change as a substitute for a control is wrong.

6.4The Usage Policy, and the high-risk list

Two separate things sit under the Usage Policy and the exam tests the second one more.

Universal restrictions cover CSAM, weapons, critical infrastructure attack, malicious cyber activity, fraud, election interference and privacy violation. Separately, High-Risk Use Case Requirements apply to consumer-facing outputs in legal, healthcare and mental health, insurance, financial and lending decisions, employment and housing eligibility, academic assessment, and automated journalism. Those require review by a qualified professional plus disclosure that AI was involved. The 2025 update confirmed these apply to consumer-facing contexts, not pure B2B. Consumer-facing chatbots must disclose AI at session start.

Two reasons, one control. For a consumer-facing decision in one of those sectors, qualified human review is required by regulation and by the Usage Policy independently. An option that satisfies only one of them still leaves the other unmet.

6.5Responsible AI as an operating habit

Four practices, and what each one actually requires
PracticeWhat it requires
Bias testing Test disaggregated slices, not aggregate accuracy. Build a counterfactual eval set: swap protected attributes and expect stable outputs. Keep monitoring after launch, because bias shows up in production distributions you never sampled. Fine-tuning is not a bias control programme
Transparency Disclose at first contact, not in the terms. Model and system cards document intended use, limitations and evaluation results, and they feed EU AI Act technical documentation rather than replacing it
Explainability, honestly bounded A chain of thought is a plausible narrative, not a faithful account of the computation, and it is not an audit trail. What you can legitimately provide: the retrieved sources, the inputs, the prompt and model versions, the policy applied, and the human who signed off
Incident response Severity levels, a kill switch that works without a deploy, rollback to the previous prompt or model version, user-notification criteria, and a post-incident review that produces a new eval case
Watch out

A kill switch that needs a deploy is not a kill switch, and an incident that does not generate a regression test will recur. Both are versions of the same requirement: the system has to be able to get back to a known-good state, and to remember that it once could not.

Treat the vendor posture as a set of facts you inherit and the practices as work you own. The exam consistently rewards architects who can say which is which.
