Six takeaways, and one idea running under all of them: the design that keeps the ability to undo is the one that survives both an incident and an exam item.

The six takeaways
#TakeawayThe operative detail
01Proportionality is the skill being testedControl cost tracks expected loss, severity multiplied by likelihood, not the loudest stakeholder's anxiety. Maximum control is a wrong answer when the stem gives low severity, reversibility and cost pressure
02Depth means independence, not countTwo layers that fail for the same reason are one layer. A prompt plus a model refusal are correlated; a prompt plus a hard tool boundary are not
03Reversibility decides where the human goesBlast radius breaks ties. Irreversible and externally visible means a synchronous gate on the action. Reversible means build the undo first, then sample. Approve the plan, not every step

8.2The distractors, by the sentence they hide behind

Twelve wrong answers that read as diligent
The optionWhy it fails
"Strengthen the system prompt" against injection or exfiltrationPrompts are not a security boundary. The right answer lives in permissioning

"Fine-tune to remove bias"Changes behaviour, is not a bias control programme. You still need disaggregated testing and monitoring

8.3How these items are built

Four structural tells worth knowing before you read the options.

  • Select TWO or THREE items in this domain usually pair one preventive control with one detective control, or one technical action with one stakeholder action. If both your picks are technical, or both preventive, re-read.
  • When two options are both correct in isolation, pick the one that matches the lifecycle stage named in the stem. A control that is right in production is often wrong during discovery.
  • "Immediately" in an option is often a tell for an over-reaction. "Before proceeding" often flags the correct sequencing answer.
  • Anything you cannot verify against a primary source is directional. The two areas most likely to have moved: EU AI Act sub-deadlines, and the exact list of Anthropic features and models eligible for ZDR.

8.4Closed-book self-test

No scrolling. Anything you cannot produce cleanly is the section to reread, not the whole module.

  1. What decides where the human gate goes, and what breaks a tie? Give the pattern for an irreversible external action and for a reversible one.
  2. For a multi-step agent, what do you ask a human to approve, and why is step-by-step confirmation worse than it looks?
  3. Untrusted third-party content arrives from a fetched web page. Where does it go in the request, how is it encoded, what is declared about it, and what happens before the model acts on it?
  4. What is the fix for confused deputy, and what is the non-fix that appears alongside it?
  5. Name what ZDR does not cover, the feature classes it is incompatible with, and the one scenario where reaching for ZDR is the wrong answer.
  6. State inference_geo's two values, which models it applies to, the price multiplier for US pinning, and what workspace geo governs instead.
  7. Walk the EU AI Act dates in order: prohibitions, GPAI, transparency, Annex III high-risk, Annex I. Then state the DPDP breach-reporting window and the minimum security-log retention.
  8. Name the four NIST AI RMF functions, say which is cross-cutting, list the columns of the risk register, and say who signs off the residual risk.

Two more worth adding if the first eight came easily: reproduce the obligation-to-control table from section 5 from memory, and name the five guardrail layers with what each one cannot catch.
