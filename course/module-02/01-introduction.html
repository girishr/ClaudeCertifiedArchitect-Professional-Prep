What changes in production

What the two heaviest domains on the paper are actually testing, and the order this module attacks them in.

Exam domains 3 and 4From this kit's domain notes
CCAR-P course / Module 2 / Section 01 of 12

A design that works in a notebook has no integration surface, no failure boundary and no evidence. It calls one API, the data is whatever you pasted in, the tools are three functions you wrote that morning, and "it works" means you liked the last four outputs. Production removes all three of those comforts at once. The capability now has to be reachable by other systems, it has to fail in a bounded way when something it depends on breaks, and somebody is going to ask you to prove it is better than what they had.

This module is those three problems. It carries 35 percent of the paper across two domains, which is more than any other module, and the two halves are tested in genuinely different registers.

The two domains and what each one is really asking
DomainWeightWhat it is really testing
3. Integration19%Not "name the MCP primitives" but "given this constraint, which coupling mechanism is correct, and what breaks if you pick wrong". Almost every scenario hands you a constraint that eliminates three of four options: the tool is stateful, the data changes hourly, there are 400 tools, a third party owns the API, the corpus is 80k tokens. Find the constraint, then pick. The second recurring theme is blast radius: who holds the token, what the tool can destroy, what happens when a tool result contains attacker-controlled text
4. Evaluation, Testing & Optimization16%Whether you attach the right measurement to the right failure, and whether you reach for the cheapest fix first. Scenarios read like incident reports: quality dropped after a model upgrade, cost tripled, the agent completes tasks but takes twelve tool calls. Wrong answers are real techniques applied too early (fine-tune before prompt work) or at the wrong layer (few-shot examples to fix a retrieval miss)

Together that is about 22 questions on a 63-question paper, and five or six of them will be multiple-response. The multiple-response items cluster in Domain 4, around "select TWO metrics" and "select TWO grading methods", so the lists in sections 8 and 9 are worth holding in memory rather than reasoning out on the day.

Watch out

Several confident-sounding answers in this domain were correct eighteen months ago. MCP sampling, an initialize handshake, connection-scoped sessions and Dynamic Client Registration all appear in third-party exam guides and all are behind the current spec. Section 3 and section 5 flag each one where it comes up, because the stale answer is built from exactly these.

One habit to carry through the whole module: read every scenario for the sentence that eliminates options rather than the sentence that describes the goal. The goal is usually generic. The constraint is the question.
