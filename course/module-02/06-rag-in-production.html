RAG in production

The measured gains from contextual retrieval, and the three cases where the right answer is not to build a pipeline at all.

Exam domain 3From this kit's domain notes
CCAR-P course / Module 2 / Section 06 of 12

Module 1 settled how a corpus is chunked and indexed. This section is the production question: given that you have a pipeline, what actually moves the failure rate, and how do you tell whether you needed the pipeline in the first place. Two of the three most common retrieval answers on the exam are refusals to build.

The baseline pipeline is: chunk at a few hundred tokens, embed, retrieve top-K, rerank, generate with citations. Everything below is a delta on that.

6.1Contextual retrieval, with the numbers

The numbers are Anthropic's own measurements at top-20 against a 5.7% baseline failure rate. They are quotable and they get quoted.

Failure rate at top-20, and the reduction against baseline
ConfigurationFailure rateReduction
Embeddings plus BM25, baseline5.7%-
Contextual embeddings3.7%35%
Contextual embeddings plus contextual BM252.9%49%
Plus reranking1.9%67%

The mechanics are simple enough to state in one sentence: prepend 50 to 100 tokens of chunk-specific context, generated by a cheap model that has seen the whole document, before both the embedding and the BM25 indexing. One-time cost is about $1.02 per million document tokens with prompt caching on the source document.

The retrieval parameters to hold
ParameterValueWhy
Chunk sizeA few hundred tokensThe retrievable unit
Context prefix50 to 100 tokens per chunkMakes the chunk findable without the rest of its document
Retrieve, then rerankTop-150 down to 20Top-20 beat top-10 and top-5 in their tests, subject to your context budget
Hybrid, not eitherEmbeddings plus BM25Embeddings capture semantics, BM25 captures exact lexical matches: error codes, SKUs, identifiers
Watch out

Two plausible fixes that are usually wrong. "Increase top-K to fix retrieval" fails whenever the miss is lexical or the chunks are context-poor, because more of the wrong thing is still the wrong thing. "Chunk smaller to improve recall" is worse: smaller chunks lose the context that made them retrievable. Add chunk-level context instead.

The situationDo this insteadBecause
Corpus under roughly 200k tokensInline it with prompt caching. This is the cache-augmented approachFaster, cheaper, and there is no retrieval failure mode to debug
A handful of navigable filesGive the agent search and read tools and let it retrieve just-in-timeAnthropic's context-engineering guidance favours just-in-time retrieval for many agent workloads, with a hybrid of a small core upfront plus runtime exploration as the pragmatic middle
Answers must reflect the live system of recordCall the APIAn index is a stale copy by construction. Freshness measured in seconds is not a retrieval problem

6.3Evaluate retrieval separately from generation

Measure recall@k and retrieval failure rate against a labelled query-to-chunk set, independently of the final answer. This is the single most useful thing you can do to a RAG system, because it splits one vague complaint into two different bugs with two different owners.

The diagnostic that follows from it: high recall plus wrong answers means the problem is the prompt or the model, not the index. Any option that reaches for a retrieval change in that situation is at the wrong layer. Section 11 turns this into a full decision tree.

So the production posture on retrieval is: measure the index on its own, add context to chunks before adding more chunks, go hybrid before going bigger, and check the corpus size before you build anything at all.
