Decomposition

Splitting a request across Claude, existing systems and humans.

Official screens S07 to S10~21 min in the original
CCAR-P course / Module 1 / Section 04 of 12

You already make three decisions when you architect anything: what the ask is, which systems you have, and where human judgment belongs. Claude adds a fourth. Over-assigning to Claude is named in the module as the most common and most expensive early mistake.

The three owners, in full wording
Three owners
OwnerWhat belongs here
ClaudeWork that benefits from language understanding, summarisation, planning, drafting, or tool-mediated action
Existing systemsAnything the partner has already paid to make reliable: the order-status service, the policy engine, the rules table, the database of record
HumansJudgment calls, exception paths, approvals, the moments where being right matters more than being fast

4.1The delegation test

Decomposition produces a delegation map. For each part you decide not whether Claude can do it but whether Claude should own it: AI-appropriate, human-retained, or collaborative where Claude drafts and a person decides. Three questions justify every assignment.

  • Reversibility. Can a wrong call be undone?
  • Stakes. What does a wrong call cost?
  • Accountability. Who has to answer for it?
The framing shift the module is building towards

Stop asking where can Claude help? Start asking where do the four properties argue for Claude over the system that already does this correctly? The first question has no wrong answer, which is why it produces bad architectures.

4.2Worked: a claims triage assistant

A partner asks for an assistant that reads a claim, decides priority, looks up policy coverage, and emails the adjuster. The instinct is to put all four in the Claude bucket. The four-properties lens stops that.

One request, four different answers
StepOwnerWhy
Read the claimClaudePattern-rich language work. With a constrained output schema, next-token prediction and steerability both work in your favour
Decide priorityExisting systemPriority is a deterministic rule the partner defines and maintains. It lives in a rule engine, not in training data. Claude calls the engine
Look up policy coverageExisting systemSame problem, higher stakes. Coverage tables change and the model cannot know when its version stopped being current. Tool use or MCP against the live source
Email the adjusterSplitDrafting is Claude. Sending is the email system. Above a value threshold a human approves, because working memory and steerability are both risks on an irreversible send

4.3Watch out: the deterministic check that quietly drifted

A partner had a rule: any claim over five thousand pounds needs a senior adjuster. It was an SQL check against the claims table. Could Claude do it instead? The architect said yes, we can prompt Claude to extract the amount and route on it, one step instead of two, simpler. The partner agreed.

Three months later: of 14,000 claims, 41 routed incorrectly. All 41 shared one trait. The amount was not a clean number. It was inside a sentence, something like damages estimated around five thousand pounds. The model treated an approximation as an approximation rather than as a figure that trips the threshold. The rule was precise. The input was not. The model followed the letter and missed the intent.

Two things made it worse, and both are the actual exam content:

  • Nobody built test cases for the routing, because routing had been reclassified in everyone's head from a rule the business depends on to something the model handles. A rule the business depends on gets tested, watched and owned. Something the model handles gets left alone until it breaks.
  • Monitoring could not see it. The logging that would have caught a broken SQL check does not record the choices a model makes inside a single request. An audit found it, not the system.

A field-service partner wants an on-site assistant for engineers. Among the requested capabilities: return the current stock level of part 78-A at the nearest warehouse, summarise the engineer's case notes into a one-page handover, approve a refund above two thousand pounds when the engineer asks, and extract the part number from a photo of the unit label.

Which two of those belong outside the Claude bucket?

  1. Summarise the case notes
  2. Return the current stock level
  3. Extract the part number from the photo
  4. Approve the refund above two thousand pounds
Stock level and the refund approval. They fail different tests, which is the point of the drill. Stock level fails the knowledge-boundary test: it is live state and belongs to the system of record. The refund fails reversibility, stakes and accountability at once. Claude can draft the refund request and explain it; a person signs it.
