The module closes with five takeaways. Notice how much of it is restraint: decompose before designing, use the known shape, start at the middle tier, choose by the work.

The five takeaways
#TakeawayThe operative detail
01Decomposition comes before architectureSplit the work into Claude, existing systems and humans first. Designs that skip it force Claude into work another system does cheaper, or ask it to operate without context a colleague would have been given
02Choosing a pattern is choosing how much autonomy to grantNot how clever the solution is. Five factors decide: predictability, error cost, observability, latency, cost. The tightest constraint is the one that picks
03Reach for a tested reference architecture before inventing oneAgent, RAG, document pipeline, routing, coding agent. Combine them when parts of the system break differently. The recurring mistake is retrieval standing in for live state
04Start with Sonnet and treat every swap as a releaseAn eval set that defines better, and a rollback criterion set before the swap. The same principle governs context: progressive holds up over a long-lived deployment, monolithic grows until it breaks
05Pick the entry point by the work, not by what is on the shelfEach carries a different core tradeoff. The right recommendation is the one whose tradeoff you can name out loud at the moment you make it, because that is what tells you later when to switch

12.1Closed-book self-test

No scrolling. Anything you cannot produce cleanly is the section to reread, not the whole module.

  1. Name the four properties an architect designs around, and one design consequence of each.
  2. Name the three layers of the platform map, and place Bedrock, MCP and Claude Code.
  3. Name as many of the seven primitives as you can, with the one-word job each does.
  4. What are the three owners in a decomposition, and what three tests decide what Claude gets?
  5. Name the four workflow sub-patterns and one situation each is for.
  6. Walk the five factors for pattern selection in order, and say what each rules out.
  7. What is the most common reference-architecture mistake, and how do you spot it in a scenario?
  8. Choose a chunking approach for a section-numbered contract and for long-form prose, and say why.
  9. What is the default model tier, and what must happen before you change it?
  10. Where does the cache breakpoint go, and what happens if you get it backwards?
  11. Name three constraints that eliminate an entry point before cost is discussed.
