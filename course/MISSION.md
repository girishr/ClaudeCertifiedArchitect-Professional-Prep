# Mission

## Why I am learning this

To pass the **Claude Certified Architect - Professional (CCAR-P)** exam, and to be
able to do the job it certifies: walk into an ambiguous business problem and walk
out with a defensible Claude architecture that survives contact with production,
compliance and a sceptical stakeholder.

## Who I am

- Engineering manager, leading a mobile team of around 50 developers
- 10+ years in technical leadership, full-stack development and product management
- Already build heavily with Claude Code day to day, including my own tool
  (SpecPilot) layered on top of it
- Comfortable with CLI, TypeScript and Python; do not need beginner material

## The real-world stake

This is not a badge exercise. Three things ride on it:

1. **Client work.** Architecting Claude solutions for clients means being the person
   in the room who can say "workflow, not agent, and here is why" without hedging.
2. **Team leverage.** Fifty developers adopting AI-assisted development need
   standards someone has actually thought through.
3. **SpecPilot.** The product is a guardrail layer on Claude Code. Every pattern
   this exam tests is a design decision the product either makes or exposes.

## What success looks like

- **Near term:** pass CCAR-P, target sitting early September 2026
- **Deeper:** given a scenario with a latency SLA, a compliance regime and a budget,
  reach for the *least complex architecture that clears the constraint* by reflex,
  not by reasoning it out from scratch each time
- **Hardest:** be as fluent in the consulting half (discovery, expectation setting,
  handoff) as in the technical half. This is the acknowledged weak side

## Known weak areas

Self-rated at the start, to be revised as learning records accumulate:

- Integration depth and MCP internals
- Evaluation practice: building eval sets, choosing graders, diagnosing root cause
- Governance, safety and compliance mapping
- Stakeholder communication and lifecycle management

## Constraints on teaching

- Short sessions. Real job, real team, limited evenings
- No beginner scaffolding. Skip what an API key is
- Bias toward *decision* practice over recall of terminology
